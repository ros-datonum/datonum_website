The limits

What we will not do

Every audit we deliver includes a do-not-automate list. It is usually the section clients find most useful.

    We will not automate something because it is technically possible. It has to pay back. We will not remove the person from a decision that needs judgement. We will not sell you an implementation if the audit says the timing is wrong. We will not replace the systems you already run. We work with them. We will not leave you dependent on us. Everything is documented and handed over.

This works when

    You are a service business of roughly 10 to 80 people. The founder or owner still makes the decisions. There is no developer, no CTO, and no one internally who can evaluate this. Work repeats often enough that fixing it matters.

This does not work when

    You build software. You have the people to do this yourselves. You are looking for the cheapest hourly rate. You want proof of return before any paid work begins. Nothing in the business repeats.

Lite

One job off your team

Best when the task is well defined, happens often, involves reading, writing, sorting, or summarising, and touches one or two of your systems.

Examples: call recordings turned into follow-up notes · enquiry forms turned into structured briefs · incoming requests sorted, prioritised, and drafted · long documents turned into the points that matter.

Standard

A whole process rebuilt

Best when an entire process is slow, not just one task. Information gets re-entered, handoffs get missed, and the same summary gets written three times by three people.

Examples: a sales call turned into a complete CRM record, follow-up email, and proposal angle · a signed contract triggering the project brief, missing-information list, and kick-off agenda · weekly project reporting assembled across your systems · enquiries turned into scoped draft proposals.

Growth

The process that runs your business

Best when one process carries real commercial weight and getting it wrong is expensive. Usually the process the founder has tried to fix twice already.

Examples: enquiry through to signed contract, end to end · delivery risk and margin monitoring across your project, time, and finance tools · the full client reporting cycle · case and claim handling from intake to resolution.

Where to start

Why this exists

An AI-assisted workflow is not a machine you install and forget.

Real use changes things.

    Your business changes — new services, new clients, new document formats. The tools you use release updates. Output quality drifts as the kinds of work coming in shift. Your team finds small things that would make it noticeably better.

Without someone maintaining it, a workflow quietly degrades until people stop trusting it and go back to doing the work by hand. That is the most common way an AI project fails — not with a breakdown, but with a slow loss of confidence.

The limits

What is not included

    Building entirely new workflows — those are quoted as implementation work. Major rebuilds or redesigns. Out-of-hours or on-call support. Unlimited changes.

Anything outside scope is quoted separately, and we will always tell you before doing it.

How we work

We measure before we build.

Most AI projects fail for one of three reasons: the wrong thing was automated, nobody checked the output, or nobody maintained it. Our approach is built around avoiding those three.

01Diagnosis before building.

We will not quote you for a build until we understand the process. If you come to us knowing exactly what you want built, we will still spend a day checking that it is the right thing.

02A person always reviews.

AI drafts, sorts, summarises, and checks. People approve. We do not build workflows that make consequential decisions unsupervised, and we will say no if you ask us to.

03Your systems, not ours.

We work with the tools you already pay for. You will not end up on a platform you can only leave by starting over.

04Tested on your real work.

Every workflow is tested against your own historical examples before it goes live. Sample data proves nothing.

05Handed over, documented.

Written guides, training for the people who use it, and a support window. If you never speak to us again, the workflow still runs.

Plainly

What we are honest about

    AI gets things wrong. The design assumes it, which is why review steps exist. Not every process is worth automating. Roughly a third of what we look at is not. The first version is rarely the final one. That is why implementations include a stabilisation period. Savings show up as capacity, not usually as headcount reduction. Most of our clients redeploy the time rather than remove the person.

About

Datonum exists because most AI advice is written for companies that do not need it.

The story

The useful writing about AI in business is aimed at organisations with engineering teams, data functions, and someone whose job is to evaluate this. That describes almost none of the businesses actually doing the work — the recruitment firm, the accountancy practice, the contractor, the freight forwarder.

Those businesses have the same repetitive load and none of the internal capability to address it. They get approached by vendors selling excitement and by consultancies selling strategy decks. Very few people show up, look at how the work actually moves, and say plainly what is worth doing.

That is the gap Datonum works in.
